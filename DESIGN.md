# Design System: CellShift AI

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The illuminated workbench"**

A compact engineering interface gives each part of the review a distinct color identity. Layered navy in dark mode and warm stone with porcelain surfaces in light mode frame a vivid synthetic network. The topology has the most visual space; colored header bands make proposal, comparison, evidence and qualitative review easy to find.

This records the implemented interface following the user's second-pass direction. Source tokens come from app/globals.css, font registration and initial theme from app/layout.tsx, and interaction states from the components. The surface contract and review evidence live in .impeccable/surfaces/workspace.md. No approved image composition is claimed.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Complete dark and light palettes, including map geometry, fields, status and overlays.
- Cyan network, amber proposal, blue metrics, coral evidence and violet review bands.
- Visible sampled coverage and a user-controlled spatial schematic.
- Pausable selected-tower pulses, aligned values and explicit confirmation.
- Plain AI status with detailed provenance retained in exported evidence.

## Colors

The frontmatter lists dark defaults without a prefix and light equivalents with a light- prefix. Both sets map directly to CSS custom properties; switch the whole palette together.

### Primary

The cyan-green accent family identifies primary actions, confirmation, selection and keyboard focus. The hover, ink, soft and edge variants supply readable interactions in each theme. Favorable evidence uses the same semantic accent.

### Secondary

Network inspection uses cyan, proposed change amber, comparison blue, affected-user evidence coral and qualitative review violet. Each section uses its own foreground, soft band and edge token. Full-width header bands carry identity; icons sit on a contrasting panel-colored square. Warning amber, danger rose and reassociation purple remain outcome/status colors, always accompanied by labels.

**The section-identity Rule.** Preserve each section's colored band in both themes and across widths; an icon alone does not carry the whole hierarchy.

### Tertiary

The five site colors stay consistent across tower, coverage region and selector: North Gate cyan, Central Hub green, Harbor amber, South Park violet and Eastpoint pink. Light mode uses deeper site colors for contrast on pale map surfaces. Site identity and user-outcome categories have separate legends and meanings.

### Neutral

Page ground, elevated shell, panel, inset, field, popover and map surfaces each have their own theme token. Primary, secondary and muted text preserve a readable hierarchy. Ground-plane, grid, tower, selection, hover and stroke colors also change with the theme; do not leave dark literals in light components.

Light mode uses a stone canvas (#e9e8e2), a slightly lighter shell (#eeeee7), porcelain panels (#f1f1e9), inset controls (#eaece5), fields (#eeeee6) and raised popovers (#f4f3ec). A quiet sage map (#e3eae7) supports the five coverage colors. Map labels (#eeefe7) and tower fills (#f2f2ea) avoid remaining bright blocks. Restrained cyan, sand, blue-grey, rose and lavender bands retain section identity. Keep pure white for ink on dark actions and selected markers, rather than large surfaces. Navy primary text is unchanged; muted text, input borders and site colors are strengthened for contrast on these surfaces. Native range inputs have transparent backgrounds.

**The labeled-status Rule.** Pair color with a label, sign, icon or accessible state; color alone cannot convey source, outcome or selection.

## Elevation & Depth

Surface tones, borders and a modest theme-specific shadow separate panels. The dark surface shadow is (0 8px 24px #02091226); the light one is (0 4px 16px #2333290a). The Display popover keeps its stronger border and own surface, with the light surface shadow in light mode and the original (0 10px 28px #0006) in dark mode. Keep overlay controls readable over colored coverage.

The 3D schematic uses orthographic SVG projection for its ground plane, sampled coverage, site masts and user markers. Rotation, tilt, bounded zoom and reset are user-controlled; selection does not recenter the camera. The exact synthetic XY plan remains available. Tower height is illustrative. A tight geometry-aware viewBox preserves uniform XY scaling, includes label/mast/target bounds and stays fixed through rotation, site selection, filtering and Before/After. Tilt and zoom can adjust safety clearance; zoom still visibly magnifies the scene. Baseline/default zoom retains the whole field. Zooming in may crop outer field edges while all site labels and hit targets stay visible.

**The coverage-and-selection Rule.** Coverage colors describe server-sampled synthetic serving regions; tower pulses only mark the selected tower. Neither is a validated RF boundary.

Selected-tower waves animate transform and opacity on a (3s linear) loop; the second wave starts (1.5s) out of phase. A visible motion control pauses/resumes them. Reduced motion disables the wave animation and leaves a faint static cue. No continuous camera movement or KPI animation is used.

Buttons use color/border transitions (160ms ease). Fine-pointer primary-action and site-letter hover lift uses (140ms) with cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1). Coverage visibility uses the shared duration (180ms) and that easing. Pending spinners use (900ms linear). Reduced motion removes smooth scrolling and makes transitions immediate.

Run readiness changes color immediately: blending its disabled and enabled palettes briefly reduces text contrast. Its hover transform remains. Display options enter over (180ms) using the shared ease-out, opacity and a (4px) vertical offset; keyboard-triggered opening and reduced motion are immediate. Disclosure chevrons rotate over (140ms), with immediate keyboard/reduced-motion feedback. There is no added attention pulse or theme crossfade.

## Components

### Buttons

The primary action uses the accent fill and readable accent ink, weight (600), padding (10px 13px) and minimum height (44px). Run fills the inspector only when available; unavailable Run uses a readable neutral inset treatment at full opacity. Interpret uses accent/soft/edge tokens. Generate/Refresh uses review tokens. JSON and Markdown exports use coordinated network/review foregrounds and borders and (44px) minimum height. Fine-pointer hover may lift primary actions by (1px); active buttons darken with brightness(0.94). Other disabled controls use opacity (0.45). Keyboard focus is an accent outline (2px) offset (4px).

### Inputs and confirmation

Textareas/selects use theme field backgrounds, visible input strokes and (6px) corners. The textarea is vertically resizable with minimum height (72px) and line height (1.7). The bounded slider advances in integer steps. Confirmation is a separate checkbox-style button with role=checkbox and aria-checked. Its target reaches (44px) on mobile; running remains a separate action. Edits revoke confirmation, and pending requests disable edits. Describe mode needs a validated proposal; clarification blocks confirmation and simulation.

**The explicit-action Rule.** Interpretation, confirmation and running remain separate actions. Selection never implies approval.

### Navigation and theme

The rail uses named icon links with an accent selected state and aria-current. Mobile removes the rail while preserving document order and a keyboard skip link. The three-step workflow groups Propose, Confirm & run, and Review impact. Review becomes current only when a computed outcome exists; running stays in Confirm & run. Start a change links directly to the proposal; View results appears locally after successful computation. No automatic scrolling is forced. The topbar retains the theme switch and reset; it does not advertise provider configuration. The theme action names its destination. A non-secret theme cookie supplies the initial server-rendered choice, preserving the selected theme on reload; dark is the default for missing/invalid values.

### Source chips and notices

Ordinary screens use AI assisted only after a validated live response, Local assistant for a local response, Local fallback when AI is unavailable, and Not generated before any call. Tooltips describe these states in plain language. Provider/model names and exact provenance stay in API/export metadata. Draft, Interpreting, Confirmed, Running and Simulated reflect real state. Errors use a dismissible in-flow notice with role=alert; fallback and result announcements use live/status regions. A short prerequisite hint accompanies confirmation/Run. Valid interpretation focuses confirmation, clarification focuses the instruction, and Edit instruction restores its field focus.

### Topology and coverage

The network panel has a cyan header band, compact 2D/3D and Before/After controls, and a separate Coverage toggle. Existing 3D camera controls are grouped horizontally in the toolbar, outside the plot. The context row and existing selected-site/user readout also sit outside the drawing; the readout shares the existing footer with the pulse explanation. After is unavailable before a result. Native site buttons complement focusable SVG targets with Enter/Space support; compact SVG hit radii are (66 units), keeping targets at least (44px) across supported camera bounds. Display reveals user filters, connection visibility and 3D tilt. Links start off. Hover/focus on an evidence row highlights its user on the map.

Coverage is sampled on the server through the unchanged engine with a (25 by 25) grid of (625) probes at (40 synthetic-unit) spacing. It is independent of the one-hundred-user KPI sample. The client groups and projects returned tiles/edges for either view; it does not calculate RF values. Unselected coverage fill opacity is (0.16), selected fill (0.29); selected boundaries strengthen from (1) to (1.8) SVG units. The persistent note says pulses mark the selected tower, keeping signal motion distinct from sampled coverage. The coverage field follows baseline/approved result state.

### Metrics, evidence and review

The metrics section has a blue heading band. Four divided KPI cells use aligned values with Baseline/After context and explicit units; an unavailable baseline uses an em dash. Evidence has a coral band, review violet. They appear after a run; evidence starts with counts, review signals and the first eight affected users, with expansion available. A visible hint explains map highlighting on hover/focus. Review generation is an explicit action. Generate/Refresh and Download evidence stay above optional review prose. Export scope distinguishes numerical evidence from a generated qualitative review; download-start feedback is visible and announced. Exports pause during requests. Observation markers are neutral, including negative numerical changes. Validation/rollback content remains expanded and readable; optional persistence appears only when configured.

### Supporting disclosures

Necessary simulation limitations and session activity remain in native details/summary controls. Keep engineering caveats useful and concise; avoid provider branding or implementation commentary in the task flow. Baseline uses one compact results placeholder.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** preserve complete light/dark palettes and the five section bands.
- **Do** match site colors across coverage, towers and site selectors.
- **Do** distinguish sampled coverage from the selected-tower pulse cue.
- **Do** preserve pause, reduced motion, keyboard control and mobile 3D.
- **Do** keep explicit confirmation separate from running, with aligned evidence values.
- **Do** use plain AI/local/fallback labels while retaining exact provenance in exports.

### Don't:

- **Don't** present schematic height, sampled edges or pulse rings as validated RF coverage.
- **Don't** invent numerical evidence in the client or through AI.
- **Don't** infer approval from interpretation, selection or a previous draft.
- **Don't** replace this topology and comparison workspace with a chat layout.
- **Don't** introduce automatic camera motion or animate numerical evidence.
